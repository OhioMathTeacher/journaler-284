Where your work lives

Saved in this browser as you type — not on a server, not in an account. It is kept by this browser on this device, and nothing else knows about it. Clearing browser data erases it, and lab machines get wiped.

⚠ Safari deletes this after a week

Safari — on iPad, iPhone and Mac alike — erases a website’s stored data if you have not opened that site in seven days. It does not warn you, and the app cannot stop it from the inside. Read in Week 1, take a week off, come back to an empty Journaler.

The fix takes two minutes

Add Journaler to your Home Screen. Safari stops treating it as a website, and the seven-day rule no longer applies.

  • iPad or iPhone: open Journaler in Safari, tap Share (the square with an arrow out of the top), scroll down, tap Add to Home Screen, then Add. From then on, open it from that icon rather than a Safari tab.
  • Mac: Safari’s File → Add to Dock does the same job.
  • Chrome, Edge or Firefox: nothing to do — they do not sweep storage this way.

The Home Screen copy starts with its own empty storage, so it opens blank at first. If you have work in Safari already, run ⤓ Save my work there first, then open the new icon and load that file with ⤒ Open my file.

Back it up anyway

⤓ Save my work downloads one dated file holding everything — your writing, your notebook, every marked passage. Keep it in Drive, Dropbox, or on a thumb drive, and reopen it with ⤒ Open my file on any computer. A backup is the only copy no browser can decide to throw away.

How often?

After anything you would not want to write again. Journaler reminds you when enough has piled up since your last one, but the reminder is a safety net, not a schedule.

Two devices?

Each browser keeps its own copy and they do not sync — read on an iPad and write on a laptop and you have two half-finished notebooks. ⤒ Open my file offers to add a file to what is already here rather than replacing it, so you can carry one device’s work to the other and end up with both.

Loading the same file twice adds nothing the second time; it can tell what it already has. Only something genuinely changed in both places is ever a question, and it asks once for all of them rather than once each.

Your readings are not in that file by default and do not need to be — reload the same chapter files with ＋ Load readings and your highlights reattach to them by filename.

No account, no server

There is nothing to log into and nothing to sign up for. Your writing never leaves your machine unless you send it somewhere yourself. There is no database of student work behind this app, because there is no database at all.

Why that matters here

A writer’s notebook only works if it is candid, and candour needs privacy. You should be able to write something half-formed, or unflattering, or wrong, without wondering who can read it later. Nobody can — not the instructor, not the university, not us.

The other half of the bargain

Work that lives only on your device is work only you can lose. There is no “restore from the cloud” here, because there is no cloud. That is the trade, made deliberately: you own it outright, and backing it up is yours to do.

One file, no lock-in

That backup is ordinary JSON inside an ordinary zip. It is readable without this app and will outlive it. Nothing here is a format you need our permission to open.

No build step, no CDN

The whole app is a handful of plain files served as they are. It runs with the network off, on a school laptop, on a borrowed iPad, in ten years. That is a deliberate limit, not a missing feature.

Optional, and off by default

Journaler works completely without it. Nothing in the course requires the AI reading partner, and no part of your grade depends on having used it.

What leaves your machine

A local model keeps everything here — nothing goes out at all. A hosted model receives what you ask about, which includes the passage or the page you are asking about. Nothing else is sent: not your notebook, not your other readings.

Marking is not asking

Highlighting a passage and writing what you think about it is note-taking, and it stays note-taking. Ask Romano sits beside that as a separate button you can ignore for the whole term.
